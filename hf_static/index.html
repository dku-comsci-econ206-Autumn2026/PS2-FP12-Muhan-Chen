Trust the table, not the resemblance

Two classmates share one computer. One promises. One decides whether to trust. The computer only deals the cards.

How to sit

  1. Promiser. You see a payoff card and choose Keep or Break. The other person cannot see this choice.
  2. Hand the computer over. Look away.
  3. Decider. You see the same card and a label, SAME or DIFFERENT. You do not see what the Promiser chose. You choose Trust or Verify.
  4. The round is revealed. Points are not money. There are 12 rounds. The explanation of the equilibrium is shown only at the end.

The label says whether a displayed priority matches the Decider. It does not change anyone's points, and it cannot remove the forfeit.

Leave the code blank for a new shuffle. Nothing is uploaded. An AI does not play either role.

What the table says

These lines are the equilibria of the cards. They are not a summary of how you played.

  • Aligned card: Keep pays the Promiser 6 and Break pays 3. The equilibrium is Trust and Keep.
  • Misaligned card, no forfeit: Break pays 10. The equilibrium is Verify and Break.
  • Misaligned card, forfeit of 5: Break pays 5. The equilibrium is Trust and Keep. The Decider still gets −4 if the Promiser breaks. The 5 points are not transferred.

SAME or DIFFERENT is not a row in the table. Trust is worth as much as Verify only when the other person keeps at least 60 percent of the time. On these cards the Promiser has a strictly better action, so the equilibrium does not use that percentage.
